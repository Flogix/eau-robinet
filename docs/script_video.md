# Demo video script (3 minutes)

Target length 3:00 (the brief asks for 2 to 4 minutes). About 430 spoken words, at a calm pace of 145 words a minute.
Record the live app (https://eau-robinet.streamlit.app/) in English, full screen, browser zoom at 90%, light theme.
Before recording, open the app once so that the data is cached, and set the town page to Paris.

Each block gives the time, what to show on screen, and what to say. The numbers are those of the app; if the data is
updated, read them again on screen before recording.

---

## 0:00 to 0:20. Hook

**Screen.** Home page, top: the title "What's in my glass?" and the lede. Scroll slowly to the section "Water that makes
people ill is not a thing of the past".

**Voice.** "Every day in France, we fill a glass at the tap without a second thought. But is that trust deserved? Water that
makes people ill is not a thing of the past: outbreaks still happen. So I asked a simple question: is my tap water good?"

## 0:20 to 0:45. Context and data

**Screen.** Scroll to the four KPIs, then the yearly curve. Hover over 2025 on the curve. Briefly show the sidebar filters.

**Voice.** "To answer it, I used ten years of official tests from the French Ministry of Health: three million samples and
133 million analysis results, from 2016 to August 2026. Overall the answer is reassuring: 98% of bacterial samples and 97% of
chemical samples met the health limits. But a national average hides the places where things go wrong, so the app goes town
by town."

## 0:45 to 1:20. My town (demo: search, report, PDF)

**Screen.** Click "The water of my town". Type "Villej" in the search box and choose Villejuif, then come back to Paris.
Show the gauge and the three bars. Point at the paragraph "In short: why this score". Open the tab of the network "CENTRE"
and point at the red label "Above limit" for lead. Scroll to "Take the report with you" and click "Download the report
(PDF, French)". Show the first page of the PDF for two seconds.

**Voice.** "Here I can look up any town. Paris gets 84 out of 100, grade B. The score combines three marks: bacteria, health
chemistry and comfort, and every number is explained in plain words. Paris loses most of its points in chemistry, because of
one lead measurement above the limit in one of its four networks. And you can
download it as a PDF, in English or in French. Every sentence is written by fixed rules from the data, so no number is
invented."

## 1:20 to 1:45. Comparison and map (demo)

**Screen.** Click "Town against town": the bars of the five default cities. Point at Marseille and Lille. Click "The map of
France": hover over Aisne, then over Bouches-du-Rhône. Change "What the map shows" to "Bacteria" for two seconds.

**Voice.** "Comparing towns shows real gaps: Marseille scores 99, Lille 57. On the map, the median town score goes from 57 in
Aisne to 99 in Bouches-du-Rhône. And bacteria and chemistry do not hit the same departments at all: their correlation is
almost zero, because they do not have the same causes."

## 1:45 to 2:15. The main insight

**Screen.** Click "What we look for, what we find". Show the headline, then the stacked bars of clue 2. Hover over 2021 and
point at the annotations of the new molecules.

**Voice.** "Now the main finding. In 2021, chemical non-compliance nearly doubled. Did the water get worse? Not really.
Laboratories started searching for new pesticide metabolites, and some were later reclassified. These three families alone
explain 58% of the exceedances between 2021 and 2024. What we look for changes what we find. And the same story is starting
with PFAS, monitored routinely only since 2026."

## 2:15 to 2:40. Build your own score (demo)

**Screen.** Click "Build your own score". Move "Pesticides and their metabolites" to x3 and the nitrate threshold to 25.
Show the metrics changing and the grade bars. Click "Back to the official settings".

**Voice.** "A score is built on choices, so I let you make them. Here I make pesticides three times more severe and halve the
nitrate threshold. The letters move, but the order of the towns barely changes: the ranking is robust. Of course, these are
personal settings, not health rules."

## 2:40 to 3:00. Implications and limits

**Screen.** Click "What it all means". Scroll over the four hypothesis boxes, stop on "For a resident, and for local
authorities". End on the home page header with the URL of the app.

**Voice.** "So, is my tap water good? Almost everywhere, yes. Problems concentrate in small networks, which are tested only
a few times a year. For residents: read the report, not just the grade. For local authorities: protect small networks first,
and read every curve with the history of what is measured. The score is a teaching tool, not a health opinion: that remains
the job of the regional health agency. Thank you."

## Checklist before recording

- Interactions shown: town search, network report, PDF download, comparison, map hover and view change, personal score
  sliders and reset. The sidebar filters appear on the home page.
- Say "teaching indicator, not a health opinion" at least once (done at 2:40).
- Keep the cursor still while speaking a number, so that the viewer can read it.
- Export in 1080p; check that the sound level is even; add the link of the app in the video description.
